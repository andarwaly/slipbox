# Explanatory lookup body

A Reference note is a practical answer to a user's lookup question. It gives the
reader enough established information to understand, recognize, and use the same
reusable referent without turning into a source dossier or a personal essay.

## Required shape

- A clean H1 containing the subject name.
- A concise definition stating what the subject is.
- Precise sections chosen from the lookup need. Established characteristics, causes,
  mechanisms, examples, applications, and guidance may belong when they materially
  improve comprehension or actionability.
- Optional disambiguation when a common confusion would make lookup unreliable.

The subject kind may be a concept, framework, tool, event, or creative work.
Adapt wording to the subject and the user's inquiry; do not create subtype templates
or require fixed headings for any one kind. Functionally dependent framework steps
stay inside the framework's one natural-unit note. Organize admitted material by
conceptual logic and dependency across sources, never by source order.

## What does not belong in the body

Do not add a source-by-source dossier or turn the note into an evergreen synthesis.
Source-specific claims stay in Literature notes. Personal judgments and novel
recommendations route to Evergreen. Stable, credible disagreement may be represented
briefly when necessary; unstable identity claims and unsupported model-knowledge
additions stay out. Provenance is recorded in configured frontmatter only.

Do not expand the note into a history, tutorial, review, exhaustive taxonomy, example
collection, or general article. Established practical guidance may explain how the
subject is used; an unbounded treatment of everything related to the subject does not.

## Presentation

Use the user's explicit request first, then `.slipbox/style-profile.json`. Prose,
bullets, Markdown tables, and Mermaid are available when they represent the admitted
material accurately. Choose a table or Mermaid proactively only when it materially
improves comprehension. A visual form reorganizes the same supported content and
replaces redundant prose; it never broadens the inquiry or supplies missing evidence.

## Boundedness test

Stop when the selected sections answer the user's lookup intent with enough context
to retrieve or act on the same X consistently. Add distinctions or examples when
they resolve a likely confusion or make the explanation materially more useful.
Preserve boundedness by keeping every section about the same coherent subject and
omitting supported details that do not serve the inquiry.
