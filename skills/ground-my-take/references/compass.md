# Compass — framework

## What this is

The Idea Compass (also called the Compass of Zettelkasten Thinking), coined by
Fei-Ling Tseng and presented together with Vicky Zhao. A single idea (X) is placed at
the centre; four directions each ask a different question about it. Sascha Fast
(zettelkasten.de) classifies it as a "closed creative technique" — "questions or sets
of questions that provide a direction of exploration from a known starting point" —
and deliberately generalized its name beyond "Zettelkasten Compass" to stress it's a
portable thinking tool, not tied to any one note-taking method: "The Zettelkasten
Method provides a framework for our thinking, but does not prescribe what and how we
think."

## Layout

```
                    NORTH
                (where X comes from)
                        │
   WEST ─────────────── X ─────────────── EAST
(what's similar to X)          (what competes with X)
                        │
                    SOUTH
                (where X leads)
```

## Directions

- **NORTH — where does X come from?** Its origin, its parent category, what caused it.
  Not a question to ask fresh — the answer is already in hand: for a Take still being
  drafted, it's whichever notes were named, retrieved, or surfaced from the backlog to
  start this session; for an existing evergreen note being revisited, it's that note's
  own `derived-from` frontmatter field, read directly from the file. Never a database
  query either way.

- **WEST — what is similar to X?** What other disciplines already hold this idea, what
  other ways exist to say or do it. Executed via the **Connect** technique — see
  `references/connect.md` for the grounding and a worked example.

- **SOUTH — where can X lead? what does X contribute to?** Drilling into X's own
  downstream contribution — what it nurtures, what it could be the headline of — asked
  independently of whatever West or East turn up. A plain direct question, same
  treatment as North: no technique needed, just ask it and let the take develop.

- **EAST — what competes with X?** What is the opposite of X, what is it missing, its
  disadvantage. Executed via the **Challenge** technique — see `references/challenge.md`
  for the grounding and a worked example.

## Guardrail

Reach for whichever direction the conversation calls for — never all four as a
mandatory checklist. Fast's own framing is explicit that the compass is a portable
thinking tool, not a required pass: forcing every direction on every take produces a
rote interview, not a sharpened one. The four directions are also independent of each
other — West's finding and East's finding don't get reconciled here. If a direction
turns up nothing, that's a complete result for that direction, not a gap to force-fill.
